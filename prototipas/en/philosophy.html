The DIPA method

Not an enterprise-wide AI transformation. One measurable change at a time.

We do not begin with technology. We begin with the evidence required to conclude that a change was worth the investment.

From a defined constraint to an independently managed capability

Every stage produces a verifiable output. Without one, there is no basis for moving forward.

01

Business constraint

Not “we need more AI”, but a specific source of friction—for example, a proposal takes four hours to prepare.

QuestionWhat is underperforming or costing too much today?
02

Economic size

We estimate the annual cost of the problem and determine what scale of intervention is economically justified.

Illustration5 hours × 12 people × 48 weeks × €40 = approximately €115,200 a year.
NoteThis is an illustrative calculation, not a client result.
03

Baseline

We establish time, quality, volume, error rate or another decision-critical metric using a consistent method.

RuleNo baseline, no proof.
04

Work redesign

We eliminate unnecessary steps, simplify the work and only then allocate responsibilities between people and AI.

Five filtersEliminate · simplify · AI · human + AI · human.
05

Bounded pilot

We test the new design within a controlled scope, with a named owner, risk limits and explicit stop conditions.

PurposeBuild evidence before committing more capital.
06

Measurement

After the change, we measure the same metric in the same way and assess realised economic value—not activity.

DecisionScale · stop · change.
07

Capability transfer

The client retains the process owner, standards, KPIs, governance rules and learning system.

PrincipleCapability, not dependency.

Net economic value

We do not count saved hours in isolation. We assess the full value created and the full cost of the change.

Solution economics

Realised economic value = business value created − total cost of change

Value created
  • +additional revenue or margin
  • +real cost reduction
  • +losses avoided
Total cost
  • −technology, integration and implementation
  • −maintenance and human oversight
  • −additional management complexity

If a solution creates more complexity than value, it is not a good solution.

The DIPA human–AI work contract

A practical governance artefact defining the AI's task, permissions, limits and accountability.

AI Work Contract

Eight questions before granting autonomy

01What work should the AI perform?
02What specific output must it produce?
03What data may it use?
04What actions may it take?
05What is explicitly prohibited?
06When must it stop or escalate an exception?
07Who is ultimately accountable?
08How will quality and value be measured?

Three levels of AI autonomy

More autonomy is not the objective. It is granted only where boundaries, controls and accountability are clear.

LEVEL 1

AI prepares

AI analyses and prepares the material. A person takes the action.

LEVEL 2

AI acts, a person approves

AI prepares or initiates an action. Nothing proceeds without human approval.

LEVEL 3

AI operates within defined limits

AI performs a bounded part of the work independently and escalates exceptions.

Capability transfer test

If DIPA stepped away tomorrow, could your team run the system independently?

  • A named owner is in place
  • The team can operate it
  • The standard is documented
  • The KPIs are live
  • The client controls the system
